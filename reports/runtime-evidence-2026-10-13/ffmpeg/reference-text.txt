The PylonRecorder Waterworks
Think of every recording as water moving through a plant: the camera is a pump
drawing raw water, the encoder is a press that squeezes it into concentrate, and your disk is the
reservoir at the end of the line. Every flag on the command line is a fitting somewhere on this
pipeline — and each one trades between how pure the water stays (quality),
how much volume reaches the reservoir (file size), and whether any
spills on the way (dropped frames).
Water = pixel data
Pipe bore = data rate (MB/s)
The press = video encoder
Reservoir = your disk
A spill = a dropped frame

Station 01 — set in Pylon Viewer / .pfs, loaded with --cfg
The Pump House · camera sensor
The sensor is the pump. Nothing downstream can add water the pump
didn't deliver — quality is capped here; every station after this one only decides how
much to throw away. Raw flow = width × height × fps × bytes-per-pixel.
Resolution / ROI
Quality ▲Size ▲▲
Pipe bore. The single fattest lever: halving
both width and height cuts the raw flow to a quarter. Cropping the ROI to just the
arena is volume you never have to pump, squeeze, or store.
Frame rate
Quality ▲ (temporal)Size ▲
Pump strokes per second. Perfectly linear:
2× fps = 2× water. Buys temporal resolution (smooth motion for tracking), not sharper pixels.
Pixel format
Bandwidth ↔ CPU
Bayer RG8 ships one pipe of concentrate
that must be diluted (debayered) at the plant — ⅓ the transport bandwidth, more processing
later. RGB8 ships three pipes of finished water — 3× the
bandwidth on the USB/GigE hose, zero work downstream.
Exposure / gain
Size ▲ (hidden!)
Murky water is hard to press. High-gain sensor
noise looks like fine detail to the encoder, so grainy footage swells the file at the same
quality setting. Good lighting is free compression.
Station 02 — between pump and plant
The Bucket Brigade · capture buffers
Spare buckets carried between the pump and the press. When the press
hiccups — an OS stall, a busy moment — full buckets wait in line instead of spilling.
These knobs never change what a saved frame looks like; they decide whether
every frame arrives at all.
--max-num-buffer
Safety ▲RAM ▲
default 0 = keep SDK/.pfs value
Number of buckets on the line. More buckets
absorb longer stalls before water spills (a dropped frame). Costs only RAM.
--usbtweaks
Safety ▲
Wider hose fittings for USB cameras — bigger
transfer buffers so the hose itself doesn't become the choke point.
Station 03 — choose your plant
The Treatment Plant · codec & backend
Which press the water runs through — or whether it runs through one at all.
This choice sets the order of magnitude of your file size before any fine-tuning.
--gpu <n>
Throughput ▲Size ≈
default -1 = OpenCV/CPU path
Which press does the work. -1 = the CPU
squeezes (OpenCV recorder). 0+ = a dedicated industrial press on that graphics card
(FFmpeg + NVENC) — it doesn't steal workers from the camera line, so high frame rates stay
safe. All --ffmpeg-* valves below only exist on
this path.
--codec none
Quality: perfectSize ▲▲▲
No plant at all — raw water straight into
bottles. Perfect fidelity; the reservoir takes the full pump rate (hundreds of GB/hour
at machine-vision rates).
--codec mjpeg
Quality ▲Size ▲▲
Every bucket squeezed on its own (each frame is
its own JPEG). A decent ~10× squeeze and every frame stands alone — easy seeking, one bad
bucket never spoils its neighbours — but it never exploits that consecutive buckets look
nearly identical.
--codec h264
Size ▼▼▼
default
Squeeze each bucket and ship only the difference
from the one before. On a mostly-static scene — an ant arena where only the ants move —
almost every frame is "same as before", so H.264 lands 50–400× smaller at the same visual
quality.
--compression <0-100>
Quality ▲Size ▲
default -1 = codec default · OpenCV path only
The squeeze dial on the CPU press. Higher =
gentler squeeze = better-looking, bigger frames. The FFmpeg path ignores this and uses
--ffmpeg-crf instead.
Station 04 — FFmpeg / NVENC path (--gpu ≥ 0)
The Press Room · how hard & how smart
Inside the plant. These dials set how much water gets squeezed out of every
litre — and how cleverly the press decides where to squeeze.
--ffmpeg-crf
Quality ▼Size ▼▼
lower = better · +6 ≈ half the flow
The master press dial. CRF/CQ means "hold this
visual quality, use whatever flow that takes." It is the quality-vs-size trade —
every other dial in this room only refines it. On NVENC it becomes CQ (with VBR) or QP
(with constqp).
--ffmpeg-preset
Size ▼ (slower presets)Throughput ▼
NVENC default: hp (high performance)
How carefully the press works. Slow presets
search harder for redundancy — a smaller file at the same quality — but need more
machine time per bucket. The default hp is deliberately fast so the press never becomes
the bottleneck at high frame rates.
--ffmpeg-rc
Policy
auto: constqp, or vbr when CRF is set
The valve policy. constqp: fixed squeeze
pressure, output flow floats freely. vbr: aim for a target flow but flex when the scene
demands. cbr: a fixed output diameter no matter what — predictable flow, wasteful on
quiet scenes, starves busy ones.
--ffmpeg-gop / --ffmpeg-gop-sec
Size ▼ (longer)Seek/recovery ▼
default 60 seconds
How often a complete bucket is bottled
(a keyframe) instead of a difference top-up. Longer gaps = smaller files, but playback can
only start pouring from a full bucket — the 60 s default means seek points (and crash-recovery
points) a full minute apart.
--ffmpeg-rc-lookahead
Quality ▲Latency/VRAM ▲
A periscope looking N buckets downstream so the
valve can brace for what's coming — flow gets allocated where it will be needed instead of
reactively.
--ffmpeg-spatial-aq--ffmpeg-aq-strength
Size ▲▲ on flat scenes!
strength 1–15, needs spatial-aq on
Smart nozzles across the frame — but NVENC
aims them at flat regions (it lowers QP there to prevent banding). On a bright,
uniform arena that is the whole frame: in the 2026-07-13 field test below, spatial AQ
quadrupled the bitrate at the same CQ. Useful for scenes with mixed detail;
counterproductive for flat lab arenas.
--ffmpeg-temporal-aq
≈ no effect (measured)
Smart nozzles across time — spend more on the
frames where things move. On mostly-static footage the field test measured no meaningful
change on top of spatial AQ.
Station 05 — FFmpeg / NVENC path
Flow Regulation · caps & the surge tank
After the press, valves that bound the outflow. Mostly irrelevant under
constqp; they define the contract under vbr and cbr.
--ffmpeg-bitrate-mbps
Quality ▲Size ▲ (directly)
The target flow. Under vbr/cbr this is the pipe
diameter you're paying for. It literally is the file size: Mbps × seconds ÷ 8 = bytes.
--ffmpeg-maxrate-mbps
Size capQuality ▼ in bursts
The pressure-relief cap — flow may never exceed
this even in a burst. Protects the drain (disk) from spikes; squeezes quality harder when the
scene explodes with motion.
--ffmpeg-bufsize-mbps
Quality ▲ through bursts
The surge tank (VBV). Absorbs a short burst —
sudden motion, a lighting change — so the cap is honored on average without instantly
crushing quality. Bigger tank = smoother quality, wobblier instantaneous flow.
--ffmpeg-surfaces
Throughput ▲VRAM ▲
Parallel press basins — how many buckets the
press juggles at once. Pure pipeline depth: affects keeping-up, never the file.
Station 06 — end of the line
Bottling & the Reservoir · container & disk
The compressed trickle gets bottled and stored. Nothing here changes quality
or size meaningfully — these fittings decide whether the water survives storage.
--video-format mkv|mp4
Crash safety (mkv)
default mkv
The bottle. mkv keeps its seal even if
the line crashes mid-fill — the file stays readable up to the last written data. mp4
needs the cap screwed on at the end (finalization); a crash can cost you the whole bottle.
--avio-buffer-mb
Write smoothness ▲
default 4 MiB
The holding tank above the drain — collects the
compressed trickle into efficient gulps so the disk sees fewer, larger writes.
--min-free-space-gb--disk-check-ms
Safety ▲
defaults: 20 GB, checked every 1000 ms
The float switch in the reservoir. Checks the
water level every second and shuts the whole line down cleanly before the reservoir
overflows.
Not on the quality/size ledger but on the flow ledger: --frames caps how
long the pump runs (burst length), --stopline is the emergency shut-off lever wired to a camera
line, and the burst-mode trigger flip is the pump's ignition quirk — one extra trigger pulse at
start-up is expected, not a leak.

If files are too big, turn these — in this order
Use the difference press. --gpu 0 --codec h264 — the order-of-magnitude
decision; everything else is fine-tuning.
Shrink the pump. Crop the ROI and lower fps in Pylon Viewer (save the .pfs, load with
--cfg). Volume you never pump is free.
Turn the press dial. Raise --ffmpeg-crf in steps of 2–3; each +6 roughly
halves the flow. Stop when your tracker starts to complain, not your eyes.
Slower preset, if the press has headroom. In the field test, p4 at the
same CQ produced ~35% less flow than p3 at identical SSIM — but it raises NVENC
load, so only where utilization allows. Do not add
--ffmpeg-spatial-aq/--ffmpeg-temporal-aq on flat bright arenas —
measured 2–4× size increase (see Station 04).
Then the fittings. GOP is already cheap at 60 s (keyframes were 0.8% of the field-test
file), rate caps only if a downstream pipe (disk, network) demands a hard ceiling.

CQ mode holds quantization
constant and lets flow float, so busy colony cameras automatically spend more bits at the same CQ
(3.30 vs 2.19 Mbit/s at cq 33 in the test) — dense scenes are not starved; the coarsening lands on
background grain, not on ants or ID tags.
Live A/B pilot, 2026-07-14 (first-generation encodes, all 26 cams): fleet bitrate fell from
271 to 51 Mbit/s — 0.20×, far beyond the re-encode prediction, because at cq 30 the
quantizer sat below the sensor-noise floor and paid for grain; cq 33 crossed above it. Colony cams
0.27×, quiet cams down to 0.07–0.17×. All 52 diagnostics healthy with clean closes. Tags equally
decodable in matched 100% crops; retained temporal noise on empty floor halved (YAVG diff
0.411 → 0.205). A ~450 GB block becomes ~90–130 GB.
